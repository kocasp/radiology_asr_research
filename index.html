Abstract

Ready-to-use automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems are increasingly considered for dictating radiology reports, yet their behaviour on Polish clinical speech is poorly characterised. We benchmark 19 off-the-shelf systems (12 open configurations and 7 commercial cloud services) on 939 natural-voice recordings (2.69 h, 14 speakers) of thyroid ultrasound and head-and-neck radiology. Beyond the word error rate (WER) and character error rate (CER), we measure the correctness of radiology terminology (term error rate, TER) against general words (GWER), and errors in numbers, negations, and body side. In classical ASR, radiology terms are recognised 3–6 times worse than general words; LLM-based transcribers narrow this gap. The best open model (Whisper large-v3) misrecognises 15.8% of term occurrences but 3.1% of general words; the best cloud system (OpenAI gpt-4o-transcribe) reaches WER 5.96%, and Google Gemini 3.5 Flash has the lowest TER (6.6%). Number-formatting conventions can inflate raw WER substantially, which is why a number-normalised WER is reported alongside.

Keywords: automatic speech recognition; Polish; radiology; medical dictation; terminology; benchmark

Key findings

  1. In classical ASR, radiology terms are recognised 3–6× worse than general words in the same recordings (Whisper large-v3: TER 15.8% vs GWER 3.1%); near-misses dominate (the model hears the term but misspells it).
  2. The best overall system is OpenAI gpt-4o-transcribe (WER 5.96%); the best terminology accuracy is Google Gemini 3.5 Flash (TER 6.6%).
  3. ElevenLabs Scribe v2 spells numbers out (“2 mm” → “dwóch milimetrów”), inflating raw WER to 28.27%; after number normalisation it drops to 5.22% WER-N.
  4. Errors concentrate in numbers, negations, and body side — the clinically critical tokens — so aggregate WER alone misleads model selection.

Note on the error annotation

Texts are shown after the paper’s normalisation (lowercase, punctuation removed, dimensions split). Alignment and error types are recomputed in the browser from the normalised reference and hypothesis.

correct substitution deletion insertion number negation body side

Substitution — a different word replaces the reference word. Deletion — the reference word has no counterpart. Insertion — the model added a word that was not spoken. Numbers, negations (nie / bez / brak) and body side (prawy / lewy) are underlined in the reference.

Data and code

Recordings derive from the public ADMEDVOICE corpus (Kaggle karmarci/polish-medical-multi-source-corpus, DOI 10.34740/kaggle/ds/6698205, licence CC BY 4.0); only the natural human-voice variant is used. Audio here is served as Opus for web playback and is not the original WAV. The evaluation code, term lexicon, and per-system transcriptions and metrics are in the project repository.
